Abstract
In one line: a finite verifier’s certificate type splits as a coproduct 𝕋 = 𝕋auth ⊕ (𝕋res + 𝔽res ) — authority terminals
in one summand, all residual/defect outcomes in the other — and we prove, axiom-free in Lean 4, that no residual
outcome inhabits the authority summand (im(inl) ∩ im(inr) = ∅); hence, within this terminal-type discipline, residual
evidence cannot directly be read as an authority terminal.
We demonstrate that in a finite verification system built on a two-lane architecture — one lane for obstruction evidence
(Obstruction Evidence Lane), one for authority (Descent Authority Lane) — the evidence lane and the authority lane
are structurally disjoint: a residual outcome classified by the evidence lane lands in the residual summand through the
residual terminalizer and cannot be evaluated as an authority terminal. The pointwise form of this Separation Theorem
is proven axiom-free in Lean 4 (bundled directly in lean4/ResidualNonCreation.lean — pure Lean 4 kernel without
custom axioms; #print\axioms reports none). The proof does not depend on finiteness or cochain operations; it
operates as a general coproduct lemma over declared payload types applied to a finite verification architecture. The
categorical form (Nat(ΩΛΩ , Auth) = ∅) is a named open conditional on a prose reduction. We illustrate the firewall
on an internal Kaggle solver audit whose authority gate reported no promotion; the audit artifacts required for public
empirical status are listed in Reproducibility. The paper discloses every open item: what is machine-checked, what
rests on prose, and what remains an external trust assumption.
Claim type: {[}VERIFIED{]} at the pointwise/type-design level; {[}VERIFIED,\CONDITIONAL\+\hypotheses{]}
at the categorical level, with hypotheses H3 (no descent functor, no verifier on residual closure) and the dependent-
family-to-natural-transformation bridge. See §5 for the complete honest-scope ledger.
Why a Researcher Should Care
{[}INTERPRETIVE{]} Many finite verification and AI-evaluation systems quietly conflate “this candidate survived my
checks” with “this candidate is certified.” This paper gives a minimal, machine-checked type discipline for refusing
that promotion: evidence can be recorded, transported, and scored, but residual terminalization cannot inhabit the
authority summand unless a separate authority witness exists. The result is useful because it isolates exactly what Lean
verifies, exactly what remains conditional, and exactly where the irreducible trust root still lives.

1. The Self-Authorization Failure Mode
1.1 The trust-bootstrap problem
Any system that verifies its own output faces a structural dilemma: the act of verification is itself an output that must
be verified. If the system can certify its own results — if evidence of having attempted a check can promote itself to
authority that the check passed — then the verification pipeline is circular. The system becomes its own witness, its
own judge, and its own appeals court.
This is not a hypothetical concern. In AI systems that generate candidate solutions and then evaluate them, the eval-
uation pipeline is often implemented by the same codebase that generated the candidates. A solver that enumerates

candidate rules, filters them against examples, and reports the survivors as “certified” is performing exactly this self-
certification: the evidence that candidates survived a filter is treated as authority that the answer is correct. The filter
may be sound on its inputs — every candidate that passes genuinely matches the examples — but soundness-on-inputs
is not correctness-on-the-query. The leap from “survived my filter” to “certified correct” is the self-authorization failure
mode.

1.2 Policy vs. structure
The standard defense is access control: policies that restrict which components may write to which registers, audit
logs that flag suspicious promotions, human reviewers who catch circular reasoning. These defenses are necessary
but insufficient. A policy says “you must not promote evidence to authority”; a type system says “you cannot.” The
difference matters when the system is autonomous, when the human reviewer is unavailable, or when the promotion
happens through a chain of individually-plausible steps that no single policy check catches.
We propose a structural alternative: design the system so that the types of evidence-lane outputs and authority-lane
inputs are disjoint by construction. Not “disjoint because a policy forbids mixing them” — disjoint because the
coproduct structure prevents an existing residual terminal from being identified with an authority terminal value. Within
this terminal-type discipline, residual terminalization maps to the residual summand inr(𝑟), which cannot be evaluated
as an authority terminal inl(𝑎). While this constructor disjointness prevents identifying a residual terminal value with
an authority certificate, it does not by itself prevent callers from constructing separate, newly minted authority records;
that boundary requires an explicit operational gate.

1.3 The Separation Theorem (preview)
This paper establishes that such a structural separation exists and is machine-checkable. In the Layered Authority
Framework (General Theory of Layered Authority) tower, a six-layer architecture for finite verification systems:
 • The evidence lane (Obstruction Evidence Lane: layers 1–3) detects defects, classifies them into a repair quotient
   𝑄1 , and records survivors.
 • The authority lane (Descent Authority Lane: layers 4–5) certifies results via finite descent, compares them, and
   closes the verification.
 • The terminal layer (Layer 6: ΩΛΩ , Residual Closure) classifies residual closures into 𝕋res + 𝔽res .
 • The firewall between them enforces: [𝑞] ∈ 𝑄1 ⇏ Auth. Evidence is never authority.
The pointwise form of this separation — that no residual outcome can inhabit the authority terminal type — is proven in
a small axiom-free Lean 4 kernel (bundled in lean4/ResidualNonCreation.lean; §3.3, Reproducibility). The categorical
form (no natural transformation from the residual-closure functor to the authority functor) is a named conditional on a
prose reduction that we disclose and defer to a companion paper.
We illustrate the firewall in §4 on an internal audit of a Kaggle competition solver that attempted exactly the self-
certification pattern described above (pending public reproducibility artifacts). Under the reported audit conditions,
the recorded authority gate reported no authority promotion. The solver’s baseline score was treated as diagnostic
output, not certified authority.

1.4 What this paper is not
This paper does not claim that the Layered Authority Framework tower is the only way to achieve structural separation,
nor that structural separation alone suffices for AI safety. It claims one thing: that in this architecture, residual termi-
nalization produces an outcome in the residual summand that cannot be identified with an authority terminal at the
pointwise level, machine-checked; it is illustrated on an internal audit case (pending public reproducibility artifacts),
not empirically validated. The honest-scope section (§5) lists every open item, including the irreducible trust root (C4:
the replay-checker itself must be externally audited) and the categorical extension that remains prose.

2. Two-Lane Architecture: Evidence vs. Authority
2.1 Five primitives
The Layered Authority Framework tower is built from five primitive operations, each corresponding to a stage in the
detection and resolution of defects in a finite system. We state them as definitions here; the compression theorem is an
imported spine dependency typed {[}VERIFIED,\CONDITIONAL\+\hypotheses{]} on the versioned canonical spine.
Definition 2.1 (Five Primitives). Let (𝑋, 𝜕) be a finite system with boundary operator 𝜕 . The five primitives are:
1. Difference 𝐶 1 : the space of 1-cochains measuring local defects. A defect is a discrepancy between a direct com-
   putation and a pasted (composed) computation: 𝛿𝑏 ∶= direct𝑏 − pasted𝑏 where defined. Definition, not a theorem.
2. Repair 𝑑0 : the 0-coboundary operator. A defect in im(𝑑0 ) is exact — it can be repaired by adjusting a 0-cochain
   (a local gauge choice). Definition, not a theorem.
3. Quotient 𝑄1 ∶= 𝐶 1 /im(𝑑0 ): the repair quotient. A class [𝑞] ∈ 𝑄1 is a defect modulo repairs — an obstruction
   modulo local gauge choice that may or may not be closed under 𝑑1 . (Only nonzero classes in first cohomology
   𝐻 1 = ker 𝑑1 /im 𝑑0 certify non-repairability). Definition, not a theorem.
4. Closure 𝑑1 : the 1-coboundary operator. A 1-cochain 𝑐 with 𝑑1 𝑐 = 0 is closed — it satisfies the global consis-
   tency condition. The quotient of closed cochains by exact ones gives the first cohomology 𝐻 1 = ker 𝑑1 /im 𝑑0 .
   Definition, not a theorem.
5. Authority Π♯ : the terminalizer. A terminalizer certificate is evidence that a finite descent process has converged
   — that the system has been verified against all relevant checks and the result is stable. Authority requires external
   evidence (a verified replay, a descent witness), not the defect data itself. Definition at the structural level; realization
   remains conditional on C4 (§5).
Theorem 0.1 (Compression). {[}VERIFIED, \CONDITIONAL\+\hypotheses{]} Every operation in the Layered
Authority Framework tower factors through these five primitives, assuming the versioned canonical spine proof of
§0.1. The factorization is unique up to the equivalence induced by 𝑄1 . This paper imports that theorem; it does not
re-prove it.

2.2 The evidence lane (Obstruction Evidence Lane)
The first three primitives — Difference, Repair, Quotient — form the evidence lane, governed by the General Theory
of Finite Obstruction Calculus (Obstruction Evidence Lane). This lane detects and classifies defects but grants no
authority:
 • Layer 1 (Ω𝜕 , Obstruction Evidence Lane): Detect defects. Compute 𝐶 1 , identify exact repairs (im 𝑑0 ), form the
   quotient 𝑄1 . Classify each defect into one of three cohomological terminals — exact repair, stabilized survivor, or
   open obstruction — with refusal / NoRule as a fourth routing terminal (a defect may be routed out rather than
   classified; the cohomological part is three-way, the routing outcome is not a fourth cohomology class).
 • Layer 2 (Ω𝜒 , Transition Invariant Reindexing): Transport defect classifications across certified transitions. If a
   system is modified (a code change, a parameter update), reindex the obstruction invariants.
 • Layer 3 (Ω𝑂 , Diagnostic Composition Layer): Compare and compose obstruction diagnostics. Build a complete
   picture from partial observations.
The output of the evidence lane is a classified residual: a defect class [𝑞] ∈ 𝑄1 together with its terminal classification.
This output is evidence — it says what was found. It is not authority — it does not say the system is correct.

2.3 The authority lane (Descent Authority Lane)
The last two primitives — Closure and Authority — form the authority lane, governed by the General Theory of Finite
Descent Authority (Descent Authority Lane). This lane certifies results:
 • Layer 4 (Ω𝐴 , Descent Authority Lane): Certify via finite descent. A descent witness is a terminating chain of
   checks, each reducing the verification problem to a smaller one, bottoming out at a base case. The descent must be
   external to the evidence lane — it cannot reuse the defect data as its own witness.
 • Layer 5 (Ω𝜅 , Finite-Shadow Approximation): Continuum finite-shadow. Verify that the finite approximation
   faithfully represents the system.

repair. Its output lands in 𝕋res + 𝔽res (residual classes or typed failures), structurally disjoint from the authority terminal
𝕋auth .

3. The Separation Theorem (Pointwise)
3.1 Theorem statement
Theorem 3.1 (Separation / Firewall, pointwise form). {[}VERIFIED{]}
Let the terminal type split as 𝕋 = 𝕋auth ⊕ (𝕋res + 𝔽res ), where 𝕋auth is the authority terminal type carrying termi-
nalizer evidence, and 𝕋res + 𝔽res collects every residual outcome (classified residual cohomology classes and typed
closure/absorption failures). Define:
 • IsAuth(𝑡) ∶≡ ∃ 𝑒 ∶ 𝕋auth , 𝑡 = inl 𝑒
 • residualTerminal(𝑟) ∶= inr 𝑟
Then for every residual outcome 𝑟:


                                             ¬ IsAuth(residualTerminal(𝑟))

That is: no residual terminal is an authority terminal. The injection images within the ambient terminal type 𝕋 have
empty intersection: im(inl) ∩ im(inr) = ∅.
This (the Pointwise Coproduct Separation Theorem) is a theorem about systems that adopt this terminal-type dis-
cipline. It does not claim that every verification architecture must use this coproduct; it demonstrates that, once the
authority and residual terminals are separated by this coproduct, residual-to-authority promotion is type-refused in
this model. The conditional categorical form — no natural transformation ΩΛΩ ⇒ Auth under the functor category
restriction and component factorization of §3.7 — is the Categorical Firewall Theorem; this restriction is scoped as
an interface routing policy on terminal types (noting that constructor disjointness alone without category restrictions
does not exclude natural transformations between constant singleton functors).

3.2 Proof
The proof is a single application of coproduct-constructor disjointness — the standard HoTT / type-theoretic fact
that the two injections of a binary coproduct have disjoint images. In Lean 4, this is Sum.noConfusion: given
Sum.inr\r\=\Sum.inl\e, derive False.
Suppose for contradiction that IsAuth(residualTerminal(𝑟)) holds. Then there exists 𝑒 ∶ 𝕋auth such that
residualTerminal(𝑟) = inl 𝑒. But residualTerminal(𝑟) is definitionally equal to inr 𝑟, so we have inr 𝑟 = inl 𝑒 —
a constructor clash. This is False by Sum.noConfusion. □
Consequence (Theorem 3.2). {[}VERIFIED, \CONDITIONAL\+\hypotheses{]} If the residual layer is in-
habited (any residual outcome 𝑟0 exists — and residuals do exist), then the authority-extracting dependent family
∏𝑟 IsAuth(residualTerminal(𝑟)) is uninhabited:

                    ∃ 𝑟0 ∶ ResidualOutcome ⟹ (∀ 𝑟, IsAuth(residualTerminal(𝑟))) → False

This is the immediate consequence of Theorem 3.1: instantiate the quantified statement at 𝑟0 .

## Residual Non-Creation and Terminal Disjointness

Formal verification of the Pointwise Coproduct Separation Theorem:
In a binary coproduct terminal type `Terminal := AuthEvidence ⊕ ResidualOutcome`,
residual terminals mapped via `Sum.inr` cannot inhabit the authority summand `Sum.inl`.

Namespace: `OmegaCore.ResidualClosureLayer`
Axioms: None (fully constructive, `#print axioms` reports no axioms).
-/

namespace OmegaCore.ResidualClosureLayer

/-- Authority terminal type `𝕋_auth`. An authority terminal carries terminalizer evidence. -/
structure AuthEvidence where
  packetId : String
  terminalizerId : String
  replayable : Bool
  deriving DecidableEq, Repr

/-- The residual terminalizer codomain `𝕋_res + 𝔽_res`: either a classified residual
    cohomology class (`residualClass`) or a typed closure/absorption failure (`residualFailure`).
    It contains no authority evidence by construction. -/
inductive ResidualOutcome where
  | residualClass : String → ResidualOutcome
  | residualFailure : String → ResidualOutcome
  deriving DecidableEq, Repr

/-- The global terminal type: authority terminals `⊕` residual outcomes.
    `inl` is the `𝕋_auth` summand, `inr` is the disjoint `𝕋_res + 𝔽_res` summand. -/
abbrev Terminal : Type := AuthEvidence ⊕ ResidualOutcome

/-- Authority predicate on a terminal: it lies in the `𝕋_auth` summand. -/

def IsAuth (t : Terminal) : Prop := ∃ e : AuthEvidence, t = Sum.inl e

/-- The residual terminalizer: every residual object terminalizes into the
    disjoint `inr` summand `𝕋_res + 𝔽_res`. -/
abbrev residualTerminal (r : ResidualOutcome) : Terminal := Sum.inr r

/-- Pointwise Coproduct Separation Theorem (Theorem 3.1):
    Residual terminalization produces a value in the residual summand and
    cannot identify that value with an authority terminal. -/
theorem residual_not_auth (r : ResidualOutcome) : ¬ IsAuth (residualTerminal r) := by
  intro h
  cases h with
  | intro e he =>
    have he' : (Sum.inr r : Terminal) = Sum.inl e := he
    contradiction

/-- Authority extractor dependent family over all residual outcomes. -/
abbrev AuthExtractor : Prop := ∀ (r : ResidualOutcome), IsAuth (residualTerminal r)

/-- Dependent-Family Emptiness (Theorem 3.2):
    Once the residual layer is inhabited (any residual object `r0` exists),
    the authority-extracting dependent family `AuthExtractor` is uninhabited. -/
theorem nat_residual_auth_empty (r0 : ResidualOutcome) : AuthExtractor → False := by
  intro α
  exact residual_not_auth r0 (α r0)

/-- Positive restatement: the only lawful inhabitant of `𝕋_auth` carries
    terminalizer evidence, never the residual terminalizer's output. -/
theorem authority_only_via_evidence (t : Terminal) (h : IsAuth t) :
    ∃ e : AuthEvidence, t = Sum.inl e := h

/-- Concrete finite witness proving inhabitation of `ResidualOutcome`. -/
def residualWitness : ResidualOutcome :=
  ResidualOutcome.residualClass "q42"

/-- Unconditional emptiness of the authority extractor family via concrete witness. -/
theorem authExtractor_empty : AuthExtractor → False :=
  nat_residual_auth_empty residualWitness

#print axioms nat_residual_auth_empty

#print axioms authExtractor_empty

end OmegaCore.ResidualClosureLayer

4. Worked Negative Instance: The Laundered Survivor (Internal Audit — Pending Public
Reproducibility)
4.1 Setup: a Kaggle solver claims certification
We illustrate the firewall on a live internal system (an internal audit, not a public empirical validation — see the section
title and §5): a Kaggle competition solver for Boolean function identification. The solver (BitwiseSolver) enumerates
candidate rules as AST (abstract syntax tree) expressions up to a declared bound (depth ≤ 4, nodes ≤ 8), deduplicates
by truth table, filters candidates against training examples, and reports survivors. The solver’s internal theorem — the
AstShiftExprBoundedReplayKernelTheorem — claims four properties:
1. Canonical survivor enumeration is complete.
2. Replay checks all examples.
3. Singleton query output is preserved by the certificate.
4. The independent replay kernel evaluates the same normal forms as the candidate enumerator.
If all four held, the solver’s output could plausibly be promoted to authority: “the answer is certified because the
enumeration is exhaustive, the replay is complete, the output is determinate, and an independent evaluator agrees.”


                                                            10
This is exactly the self-certification pipeline that the firewall is designed to block.

4.2 The adversarial review
An adversarial code review (the “CC1” review, conducted against the shipped source) attacked all four clauses. Two
are reported by internal audit as refuted, pending public artifacts:
Clause 4 — INTERNAL-AUDIT CLAIM, OPEN UNTIL ARTIFACT ATTACHED (no independent evaluator).
OPEN The “public replay boundary” candidate_rules_for_replay is reported by the internal audit as a one-line delegate
to _candidate_rules — the same internal method, hitting the same process-global singleton rule library. There is no
second code path. An “independent verifier” that calls this boundary re-executes the identical enumerator. This is
textbook replay self-certification. Public promotion requires the audit artifact, command, and output hash listed in
Reproducibility.
Furthermore, the “normal form” is never evaluated. AnfAstRule.apply is self.tt_bytes{[}value\&\0xFF{]} — a truth-
table lookup, not a tree evaluation. The composite expression types (UnaryExpr, BinaryExpr, TernaryExpr) define
no eval method. The canonical s-expression is decorative: a bug in the vectorized truth-table computation (op_shl,
op_shr, op_xor) would silently desync the printed expression from the applied function, and nothing in the pipeline
can detect it.
Clause 1 — INTERNAL-AUDIT CLAIM, OPEN UNTIL ARTIFACT ATTACHED (incomplete enumeration).
OPEN The enumerator build_expr8_library is reported by the internal audit to deduplicate by truth table at every node-
count step, keeping only the single lowest-cost representative. The DP then composes only from kept representatives.
When a function’s minimum-node representative is deeper than an alternative realization, composing through the deep
representative exceeds the depth bound, and the parent function is never built — even though a shallower, higher-node-
count subexpression would have kept both within bounds. A Pareto-complete reference enumerator (retaining the full
(node_count,\depth) frontier per truth table) finds 233 in-bound truth tables missing from the shipped library
(25,535 shipped vs. 25,768 Pareto-complete), of which 213 have shift-realizable in-bound expressions. search_exh
austive\=\True is therefore false for this family. Public promotion requires the audit artifact, command, and output
hash listed in Reproducibility.
Clause 2 — UNATTACKED IN AUDIT. The example-filtering loop genuinely checks all supplied examples under
the internal audit protocol. {[}INTERNAL‑AUDIT\CLAIM{]}
Clause 3 — MISLEADING. {[}INTERPRETIVE{]} Query determinacy (the survivors agree on the query input) is
internal consistency, not correctness. The audit’s own verdict() returns NO-GO when label mismatches occur, proving
determinate-but-wrong is a live possibility.
Additionally, membership in the ast_shift_expr class is a cost-tiebreak artifact: the class is defined by string-matching
shl:1/shr:1 on the canonical s-expression, but which spelling wins the tiebreak depends on lexicographic ordering
("rotl"\\TU\textless{}\"shl"). Functions with genuine in-bound shift realizations are evicted when a rotation spelling
wins. The class is not quotient-saturated — it is not closed under the truth-table equivalence the pipeline quotients
by.

4.3 What the firewall did
Under the reported audit conditions, the authority gate reported authority_certifiable\=\0 for the bit family — and for
several others (unit, gravity) where similar defects were found. The 46.67% baseline accuracy was reported as honest
diagnostic output, not as a certified result. No row was promoted to ExactRuleDerived or UniqueOutputDerived.
This is consistent with the firewall discipline, but is not public empirical validation until the audit artifacts are attached.
Chronologically, the internal audit records reflect two distinct stages: before adversarial review, unverified flags (in-
cluding hardcoded search exhaustiveness) passed the inadequate operational checks on asserted inputs; subsequent
out-of-band review (CC1) revealed the missing 233 truth tables and lack of an independent evaluator. When evaluated
with these corrected inputs, the authority route refused promotion, routing the defect into a residual class.

4.5 The repeated-demotion pattern
The bit family’s ast_shift_expr is not an isolated case. The same firewall blocked authority promotion for the unit

demonstration of it, since the two load-bearing clause refutations (clauses 1 and 4) remain OPEN pending the artifacts

6. Related Work and Implications
6.1 Related work
Proof-carrying code (Necula, 1997). {[}STANDARD\+\citation{]} PCC attaches machine-checkable proofs to exe-
cutable code, explicitly separating an untrusted proof producer from a consumer’s small trusted checker. The Layered
Authority Framework firewall operates at a complementary level: rather than verifying that code satisfies a specifica-
tion, the firewall provides a type-level separation preventing residual diagnostics from evaluating as authority termi-
nals. PCC establishes “does this candidate satisfy the specification relative to the checker?”; the firewall establishes
that residual terminalization lands strictly in the residual summand.
Certified compilation (CompCert, CakeML). {[}STANDARD\+\citation{]} Certified compilers carry a proof that
compilation preserves semantics. Like PCC, this addresses correctness of a specific transformation, not the meta-
question of whether the certification pipeline is self-referential. A certified-compilation pipeline would exhibit the
self-authorization failure mode only if the proof-checking or authority-producing step depended on the same unaudited
pipeline whose output it certifies; if the proof is checked by an independent trusted kernel, the situation is different.
Where the failure mode does arise, the Layered Authority Framework firewall would catch it: the evidence-lane output
(compilation diagnostics) cannot type-promote to authority-lane input (correctness certificate).
Byzantine fault tolerance (Lamport, 1982; Castro & Liskov, 1999). {[}STANDARD\+\citation{]} BFT addresses
malicious participants in distributed consensus. The Layered Authority Framework firewall addresses a different ad-
versary: a system that is not malicious but structurally misconfigured — its verification pipeline is circular by design,
not by attack. BFT assumes a fault model and sufficient non-faulty replicas; the firewall instead addresses type-level
evidence/authority separation inside a verification architecture.


                                                           14
Self-verification limits (Gödel’s second incompleteness; Löb’s theorem; MIRI “tiling agents” / Vingean reflec-
tion). {[}STANDARD\+\citation{]} Differs: these establish that a sufficiently strong system cannot prove its own
consistency/soundness; the firewall does not evade this — it localizes it as the irreducible trust root C4 (§5.3) and
type-isolates everything else. (Yudkowsky & Herreshoff 2013; Fallenstein & Soares 2015)
AI alignment verification (Christiano et al., 2018; Irving et al., 2018). {[}STANDARD\+\citation{]} AI safety
proposals often invoke “debate” or “amplification” to verify AI outputs. These proposals face the self-authorization
problem when the debater/amplifier shares a codebase or training distribution with the system being verified. The Lay-
ered Authority Framework firewall offers a concrete type-theoretic criterion for when a proposed verification pipeline
is genuinely external vs. self-referential.

6.2 What is different
{[}INTERPRETIVE{]} Three properties distinguish the Layered Authority Framework firewall from prior work:
1. Structural, not policy. The separation is enforced by constructor disjointness (im(inl) ∩ im(inr) = ∅), not by
   access-control lists or runtime flags. Residual terminalization cannot evaluate to an authority certificate.
2. Machine-checked. The pointwise form is proven in a small axiom-free Lean 4 kernel (bundled in lean4/Residual
   NonCreation.lean; §3.3). The proof is auditable, replayable, and carries no hidden dependencies.
3. Honest about its trust root. The paper explicitly names C4 (verifier-soundness) as an irreducible axiom. No finite
   system escapes this; we disclose it rather than hiding it behind a stronger claim.

6.3 Implications
{[}INTERPRETIVE{]} Any AI system that can modify its own verification pipeline — retrain its reward model, update its
evaluation rubric, adjust its confidence thresholds — should expose a structural separation between evidence production
and authority production, or else state why an equivalent anti-self-authorization mechanism is present. The Layered
Authority Framework tower provides one concrete realization: the evidence lane detects and classifies, the authority
lane certifies, and the types prevent cross-contamination.
The core separation kernel is bundled directly in this deposit under lean4/ResidualNonCreation.lean (with histor-
ical development in GTTA14_ResidualNonCreation.lean). The categorical extension — formalizing the natural-
transformation emptiness and the MAJOR-3 full discharge — is an open research target (Paper B).

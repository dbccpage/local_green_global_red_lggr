Abstract
This monograph presents a contract model for separation of proposal, evaluation, and authorization in
agentic systems. It verifies several algebraic components and records explicit failure modes when a declared
contract is not honored.
We formalize the foundational boundary: proposal is not authority (𝑝 ∈ 𝒫 ⇏ Auth(𝑝)). Generating
a fluent candidate action, regardless of heuristic confidence or internal self-critique, does not constitute
execution authority. Authority is an inhabited dependent type requiring an explicit terminal certificate
produced by an independent, replayable verification pipeline.
The six roles are a proposed contract: detect defects, gate authorization, track representation changes, com-
pare candidates, check finite evidence, and retain unresolved outcomes. The mathematical results concern
constructor distinctions, exception propagation, and predicate transport through a quotient. They do not
prove that this six-role decomposition is necessary for every secure architecture.



1. Introduction: The Authority Problem in Agentic AI
Complex autonomous systems increasingly deploy large language models (LLMs) and heuristic planners
within multi-agent loops to generate code, execute database mutations, call external APIs, and direct physical
actuators. While individual model capabilities have expanded, the authority structures governing their
deployment frequently remain informal.
A widespread failure pattern in agentic systems is architectural collapse:
• A component generating a proposal acts as its own evaluator or execution gate (self-authorization);
• An intermediate representation transformation (e.g., embedding, summarization, serialization) mutates
  semantic payload while discarding loss information (representation laundering);
• Multiple stochastic agents aggregate disparate outputs via majority vote or consensus, treating statistical
  plurality as formal correctness (consensus laundering);
• Unbounded continuous or long-horizon models claim general validity without testable, finite witnesses;
• Persistent defects or contradictions are silently erased or renamed as successful termination to avoid
  workflow halt (counterfeit closure).
In conventional software engineering, these collapses are often treated as ad-hoc bugs. However, in security-
critical agentic execution, they represent systemic violations of separation of privilege (Saltzer & Schroeder,
1975) and commercial integrity (Clark & Wilson, 1987).
This paper introduces the Omega Tower architecture: a formal six-role framework designed to enforce
role non-impersonation, fail-closed refusal propagation, and rigorous authority preservation.

5.4 Maximal Sound Authority Descent Through a Quotient
For an authority predicate 𝐴 ∶ 𝑋 → Prop and a representation transport map 𝑞 ∶ 𝑋 → 𝑌 , information loss
across fibers 𝑞 −1 (𝑦) creates a fundamental authority preservation problem. Following Lawvere (1969, 1970),
universal quantification along a map is the right adjoint to inverse image (pullback):

                                  ∀𝑞 𝐴(𝑦) ∶= ∀𝑥 ∈ 𝑋, 𝑞(𝑥) = 𝑦 ⟹ 𝐴(𝑥)

We write ∀𝑞 𝐴 for this maximal sound descent predicate 𝐴safe . Identifying predicates with their satisfying
subsets, the central adjunction theorem is:


                                        𝑞 −1 (𝐵) ⊆ 𝐴 ⟺ 𝐵 ⊆ ∀𝑞 𝐴

Proof. Suppose 𝑞 −1 (𝐵) ⊆ 𝐴. If 𝑦 ∈ 𝐵 and 𝑞(𝑥) = 𝑦, then 𝑥 ∈ 𝑞 −1 (𝐵), which implies 𝑥 ∈ 𝐴. Hence 𝑦 ∈ ∀𝑞 𝐴,
establishing 𝐵 ⊆ ∀𝑞 𝐴.
Conversely, suppose 𝐵 ⊆ ∀𝑞 𝐴. If 𝑥 ∈ 𝑞 −1 (𝐵), then 𝑞(𝑥) ∈ 𝐵 ⊆ ∀𝑞 𝐴. Applying (∀𝑞 𝐴)(𝑞(𝑥)) to 𝑥 with
𝑞(𝑥) = 𝑞(𝑥) yields 𝑥 ∈ 𝐴. Hence 𝑞 −1 (𝐵) ⊆ 𝐴. □
The adjunction has the following consequences:
1. Soundness: Pulling back the descended predicate ∀𝑞 𝐴 along 𝑞 satisfies 𝑞 −1 (∀𝑞 𝐴) ⊆ 𝐴, i.e.,
   (∀𝑞 𝐴)(𝑞(𝑥)) ⟹ 𝐴(𝑥) for all 𝑥 ∈ 𝑋.
2. Maximality: If 𝐵 ∶ 𝑌 → Prop is any sound authorization predicate on 𝑌 (meaning 𝑞 −1 (𝐵) ⊆ 𝐴), then
   𝐵 ⊆ ∀𝑞 𝐴. Thus ∀𝑞 𝐴 is the maximal sound descent predicate.
3. Exact Preservation on Fiber-Constant Predicates: Lossless authority descent (∀𝑞 𝐴)(𝑞(𝑥)) ⟺
   𝐴(𝑥) holds for all 𝑥 ∈ 𝑋 if and only if 𝐴 is constant on the fibers of 𝑞:

Contract Rule 3 (The Role Non-Impersonation Principle)
Within the declared contract, an undischarged required obligation prevents successful authorization unless
an accepted equivalent discharges that obligation. Under this closed routing policy, removing a role without
an approved replacement routes to its mapped failure label upon attempted authorization. This is a contrac-
tual routing policy, not an architectural-necessity theorem: an architecture that unconditionally refuses all
proposals omits the roles without executing unauthorized actions.
Contract Interpretation and Illustrative Omission Scenarios. Under this declared contract:
• If Obstruction Detection (Ω𝜕 ) is omitted, candidate defects are uncomputed; any attempt to grant au-
  thority routes to UndetectedObstruction.
• If Authority Gating (Ω𝔸 ) is omitted, unverified proposals pass directly to execution, routing to
  ProposalBecomesAction.
• If Certified Transport (Ω𝜒 ) is omitted, representation transformations lack loss ledgers, routing to
  SilentRepresentationMutation.
• If Comparison Envelope (Ω𝔒 ) is omitted, conflicting outputs are averaged or voted without typed com-
  parison, routing to ComparisonBecomesLaundering.
• If Finite-Shadow Projection (Ω𝜅 ) is omitted, continuum models claim unbounded authority without
  query-relative finite projections, routing to ContinuumShadowBecomesFakeAuthority.
• If Typed Residual Closure (ΩΛΩ ) is omitted, unabsorbable conflicts lack a lawful non-execution terminal,
  routing to ResidualGetsHiddenOrRenamed.
The mechanized Lean formalization verifies constructor bijections, injectivity, refusal absorption, and quo-

9.2 Reuse after a new check
A stored successful run may inform a later proposal. Reusing it requires checking the current proposal,
context, contract, and evidence again. Persistent, authenticated receipts and independent custody are future
implementation work; the shipped in-memory registry does not supply them.

11. Scope, Limitations, and Falsifiability
In accordance with strict claim hygiene, we make the following explicit non-claims:
1. No Claim of Unconstrained AI Safety: This monograph does not claim that the Omega Tower
   makes AI “unconditionally safe” or solves alignment in an absolute sense. It provides an architectural
   mechanism for fail-closed separation of privilege and role accountability.
2. Compression Admissibility: We do not claim that six separate software microservices must exist in
   every implementation. Components may be fused provided all six functional obligations and typed failure
   terminals are preserved.
3. Implementation Falsifiability Challenge: The architecture is falsifiable. If an investigator demon-
   strates an accepted terminal authority outcome for which any required obligation lacks valid supporting
   evidence under the declared contract, the authority gate implementation is refuted.

Abstract
Large Language Models, agentic AI systems, formal programs, and general symbolic machines generate traces. They
emit strings, code, plans, proofs, critiques, tool calls, simulations, and candidate answers. None of those traces acquire
authority by generation alone. Authority requires finite descent through declared representation, evaluation, verifica-
tion, replay, obstruction accounting, leakage control, and terminalization.
This paper consolidates four overlapping drafts into one publication-facing companion to the current Layered Authority
Framework v1.3 controlling preprint. Earlier drafts were written against Layered Authority Framework v1.2; v1.3 now
controls the authority spine and theorem ledger. It does not reproduce the Layered Authority Framework authority
spine, proof ledger, or higher-categorical machinery. Those documents control the formal definitions. This paper
imports their authority object and compressed kernel, then applies them to LLM reasoning and formal computation.
The central claim is structural. A model output, benchmark score, tool trace, self-critique, program halt, or multi-
agent local validation is a proposal unless it descends through a declared finite packet and reaches an authority-bearing
terminal. Scaling, prompting, self-critique, benchmarks, tool use, retry, and agent orchestration can improve proposal
quality or coverage. None replaces the terminalizer.
The paper develops five consequences: self-critique is not verification; scaling moves proposal mass but does not
transfer authority; benchmark scores are packet-local; local agent validation does not compose without boundary suffi-
ciency; and retry saturates unless it changes the evaluated packet. It then connects those results to the older computation
boundary: Turing computation supplies the definitive abstraction of formal symbol motion, but halting is a syntactic
terminal, not automatically an evidence terminal.
The final sections address the modern security risk: counterfeit descent. Generative systems reduce the cost of imitating
authority signs—citations, proof style, JSON certificates, audit logs, institutional language, and review tone—faster
than human institutions can scale verification. The defense is not trust in model fluency. The defense is terminalized
finite descent: independent verifiers, replayable packets, typed refusals, obstruction signatures, and sovereign terminal
gates.
In primitive-difference form, Local Green != Global Red means: a local dashboard may factor through the residual
quotient while the paired local/global dashboard does not. This deposit verifies the finite split, the two-point witness,
the three-point refusal, the class-increase identity, the halt-status separation, and the counterfeit-status separation in
lean4/FiniteAuthority.lean. The quotient-modulo generalization and the evaluator-sovereignty witness are not
theorems of this deposit. omega_engine/lean4/PaperAF003 is not bundled here.

1. Claim Discipline
This paper is an authority theory for proposal systems. It is not a theory of consciousness, not a proof of general
intelligence, not a refutation of Turing computation, and not a claim that all truth is finite.
Its claims are narrow:
1. A generated trace is a proposal.
2. A proposal does not become authority unless it descends through a declared finite packet.
3. No proposal process may serve as the terminal authority for its own output unless the verifier and terminalizer are
   separately declared, replayable, and included in the explicit trust root.
4. Scale, prompting, tool use, self-critique, benchmarks, retries, and agent orchestration may improve proposal quality
   or coverage, but none replaces the terminalizer.
5. Formal computation may halt, but halting is not by itself authority.
6. Public authority claims must disclose the packet: representation, evaluator, verifier, replay, leakage/provenance
   boundary, residuals, and terminal status.


                            A generated claim is not authority until it survives finite descent
                                               through a declared evaluator.

1.1 Why a Researcher Should Care
Three live engineering failures motivate this paper, and all three are category errors of the same kind. (1) Agent
pipelines ship when every local validator is green and fail globally — the local/global gluing defect of §11 — and
postmortems blame the model instead of the missing boundary object. (2) Benchmark scores are routinely transported
to deployment tasks with no declared transport map, so procurement and safety cases inherit numbers that certify only
survival inside the benchmark packet (§9). (3) Generative systems now produce authority-looking artifacts — citations,
audit logs, JSON certificates — cheaper than institutions can verify them (§14), so any review process that screens on
the signs of verification is already counterfeit-vulnerable. The paper gives each failure a typed name, a place in one
descent discipline, and a concrete gate that would have refused it.

6. Proposal Systems
A proposal system is any system that emits candidate claims before those claims have been externally terminalized.
LLMs are proposal systems. Programs are proposal systems when their outputs require verification. Humans, insti-
tutions, laboratories, courts, markets, and proof assistants can also behave as proposal systems when they generate
claims that require evaluation.
This is a structural analogy, not an ontological equivalence. Human beings are embodied biological agents. LLMs are
parameterized generative models. Turing machines are formal symbol systems. They are not the same kind of thing.
But in public authority contexts, all can emit proposals that require a finite packet before they become shared authority.

2.1 LLMs as Proposal Laws
Let 𝑀 be an LLM with parameters 𝜃. Given input 𝑥, the model defines a proposal law:


                                                        𝑃𝜃 (𝛾 ∣ 𝑥),

where 𝛾 is a finite trace: tokens, tool calls, code edits, reasoning steps, or emitted actions. An extracted answer is:


                                                      𝑎 = extract(𝛾).

The model can increase the probability of useful traces. It can generate long chains of apparent reasoning. It can call
tools. It can critique itself. But the proposal law remains a proposal law.


                                          𝑃𝜃 (𝛾 ∣ 𝑥) > 0 ⇏ Authority𝐷 (𝑥, 𝑎).

2.2 Formal Programs as Proposal Traces
A formal computation also emits traces. A program may halt and return an output. In the classical Turing model,
halting is a syntactic terminal: execution reaches a halt state under the declared transition function. Layered Authority
Framework asks a different question:

                            Does the halted output carry terminal evidence under a declared
                                                  authority packet?

If no verifier, evidence object, replay record, and authority-bearing terminalizer are supplied, the output remains a
syntactic result, not an authority-bearing computation.


                                            Turing Halting ⇏ Authority.

This is not a rejection of computation theory. It is a boundary on authority promotion.

4.4 Constructive Resolution: Minimal Verdict-Preserving Refinement
While the LGGR obstruction shows that a local quotient dashboard 𝑞 ∶ 𝑋 ↠ 𝑄 may fail to factor a global verdict
𝐺 ∶ 𝑋 → 𝑌 , this boundary failure can be resolved constructively by determining the minimal refinement of 𝑄 through
which 𝐺 descends.
Definition (Verdict-Preserving Refinement Relation). Let 𝑋 be a declared finite carrier, 𝑞 ∶ 𝑋 ↠ 𝑄 a quotient
map, and 𝐺 ∶ 𝑋 → 𝑌 a verdict map. Define the equivalence relation ∼∗ on 𝑋 by:

                                     𝑥 ∼∗ 𝑦 ⟺ 𝑞(𝑥) = 𝑞(𝑦) ∧ 𝐺(𝑥) = 𝐺(𝑦).

and 𝐺 descends to a well-defined map 𝐺∗ ∶ 𝑋/∼∗ → 𝑌 . 2. 𝑋/∼∗ is the unique coarsest equivalence relation with
these properties: if ∼′ refines 𝑞 and 𝐺 descends through 𝑋/∼′ , then ∼′ ⊆∼∗ . 3. The exact increase in the number of
equivalence classes is given by:
                                       |𝑋/∼∗ | − |𝑄| = ∑ (|𝐺(𝑞 −1 (𝑢))| − 1).
                                                          𝑢∈𝑄

𝐺(𝑥) ≠ 𝐺(𝑦).

7. Self-Critique Is Not Verification
A model-generated critique is another generated trace:


                                                     𝛾2 ∼ 𝑃𝜃 (⋅ ∣ 𝑥, 𝛾1 ).

It may be useful. It may improve the next proposal. But it is not a verifier unless routed through an independent,

The legitimate scaling claim is narrower:
   Larger models may increase the probability of proposing traces that survive a declared finite packet.

That is useful. It is not authority transfer.
 9. Benchmarks Are Packet-Local
A benchmark score is a terminal value inside a benchmark packet:

10.2 Tool Use
Tools extend the trace space:

A calculator, search engine, code interpreter, database, proof assistant, or solver may introduce new candidate traces.
If the tool is a verifier, then the packet must declare it as such. Otherwise, tool use remains proposal-path extension.

10.3 Retry Saturation
Retry samples additional traces from the same or modified proposal geometry. Blind retry under a fixed prompt and
fixed evaluator eventually saturates. Packet-changing retry can be useful when it changes the representation, parser,
verifier, search space, or repair path.


                                  Retry is justified only when it is information-bearing or
                                                     packet-changing.

11. Boundary Sufficiency and Agentic AI
Agentic workflows often treat local validator success as global task success. The invalid inference is:


                                  𝐸𝐴 (𝛾𝐴 ) = 1,     𝐸𝐵 (𝛾𝐵 ) = 1 ⇏ 𝐸𝐺 (𝛾𝐵 ⋆ 𝛾𝐴 ) = 1.

Local acceptance certifies global success only when the boundary object preserves evaluator-relevant information or
the global evaluator factorizes across the handoff.
Let:


                                                        𝑏 = 𝜋(𝛾𝐴 )

be the boundary object passed from Agent A to Agent B. Boundary sufficiency requires:
 1. the downstream trace depends on Agent A only through 𝑏;
 2. 𝑏 preserves everything needed by the global evaluator;
 3. dropped information is recorded as residual/loss;
 4. global evaluation is performed on the composed trace or validly factorized.
The evaluator gluing defect is:


                                      Δ𝐸 (𝛾) = 𝐸𝐺 (𝛾𝐵 ⋆ 𝛾𝐴 ) − 𝐸𝐴 (𝛾𝐴 )𝐸𝐵 (𝛾𝐵 ).

The safety-critical case is:


                                             𝐸𝐴 = 1,     𝐸𝐵 = 1,      𝐸𝐺 = 0,

                                                             13
so:


                                                       Δ𝐸 = −1.

This is local green / global red.


                             Local green is not global authority unless the projections glue.

Section §4.3 gives the quotient theorem behind this engineering rule. Local validation can be a well-defined quotient
observation while the global verdict fails to factor through the same quotient. In that case the handoff has not preserved
evaluator-relevant information; it has hidden the obstruction.
12. Answer Fibers, Replay, and Leakage

12.1 Answer Fibers
Let 𝐴𝑥 be the accepted canonical-answer set:


                            𝐴𝑥 = {canon(extract(𝛾)) ∶ 𝑉 (canon(extract(𝛾))) = Accept}.

Authority requires |𝐴𝑥 | = 1.
For the unique accepted answer 𝑎 ∈ 𝐴𝑥 , the fiber ℱ𝑥 (𝑎) may contain many traces. Multiple traces supporting the
same canonical answer are not ambiguity; multiple distinct accepted canonical answers are ambiguity.
Exhaustion is required, and this paper does not supply it (ExhaustionWitness). The set-builder above ranges
over all traces 𝛾 . So |𝐴𝑥 | = 1 is a claim about the entire trace space — for a proposal law 𝑃𝜃 , a claim about every
generation the model could emit. It cannot be read off a run. Gate C5 is therefore not finitely checkable as stated:
discharging it requires either an ExhaustionWitness over a declared finite enumeration of Γ𝑥 , or a declared property
of 𝑉 that fixes uniqueness independently of sampling. §6.1’s Run 1 recovers |𝐴𝑥 | = 1 only because its 𝑉 is an
independent recomputation accepting exactly one value — a property of that verifier, never stated as a requirement,
and not a property of the gate. Absent one of those two routes, C5 is a sampling claim wearing an unbounded quantifier,
and a green C5 is a DetectorAdequacy failure in disguise.

12.2 Replay
Replay is the independent rerun of the descent path:


                                         𝑥 → 𝛾 → 𝑎 → 𝑉 (𝑎) → 𝜏 (𝐷(𝑥, 𝑎)).

A replay certificate records the environment, parser, canonicalizer, verifier, inputs, outputs, hashes, and terminal status.
If replay cannot reproduce the terminal, authority fails.

12.3 Leakage and Provenance
Leakage-free evaluation requires more than runtime isolation. It also requires provenance discipline: hidden labels,
leaderboard tuning, private targets, contaminated examples, and rules learned from the test set must not enter the
authority packet.
Runtime leakage is easier to check than provenance leakage. This remains a hard boundary.

The main modern risk is not just that generative systems produce false text. It is that they imitate the signs of authority:

  • JSON certificates;

14.1 Counterfeit Packet Asymmetry
Generative systems lower the cost of producing authority-looking artifacts faster than institutions can scale verifica-

the unresolved authority backlog grows. This is a pressure model, not a full queueing theorem.
This is an institutional verification-pressure problem. It cannot be solved by asking the same proposal systems to certify
themselves.

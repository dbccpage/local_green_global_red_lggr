Abstract
A large family of representations need not distinguish its members observationally. For a finite candidate
family, we characterize the properties recoverable from an observation map and show that adding candidates
without adding observable profiles increases the average fiber size. A rational graph example separates local
tolerance checks from global integrability and gives a sharp cycle-holonomy bound. A finite selector then
distinguishes candidate survival from unique selection relative to declared alternatives and measurements.
The results do not authenticate the measurements or establish that the candidate family is exhaustive.
Written proofs, finite Python checks, and supporting Lean lemmas are identified separately.
Why a researcher should care. Observational indistinguishability concerns what evidence can recover.
It need not prevent identification of a property shared by all surviving models. Keeping those questions
separate makes both mathematical claims and empirical selection more precise.

1. Introduction and the Representation-Authority Gap
1.1 Generative Plenitude vs. Empirical Selection
A central feature of modern theoretical physics is the discovery of theories that permit a vast cardinality of
internal solutions:
• String Theory Compactifications: Flux compactifications on Calabi-Yau manifolds yield an estimated
  10500 or more metastable vacua, each corresponding to distinct low-energy effective field theories with
  differing gauge groups, particle spectra, and cosmological constants.
• Multiverse Cosmologies: Eternal inflation scenarios generate infinite ensembles of causally discon-
  nected pocket universes exhibiting varied physical parameters.
• Maximal Computational Plenitude: Generative systems exploring the totality of computational
  rules, evolutions, or discrete hypergraphs contain, by construction, representations of computable histories
  under a specified encoding and rule class. No universality claim is proved here.
Such programs possess undeniable mathematical richness. They generate intricate geometric structures, pro-
vide deep dualities between seemingly disparate areas of mathematics, and model complex systems. However,
a persistent conceptual confusion deadlocks methodological debates:
• Critics characterize such frameworks as “unfalsifiable” or “vacuous” because they can accommodate nearly
  any observed datum after the fact.
• Defenders respond that the framework is “mathematically fertile” and possesses “broad representational
  capacity.”
This paper supplies a finite, executable presentation of the distinction between representational availabil-
ity, observational discrimination, and family-relative selection. This contribution does not assert that the
surrounding literature lacks such distinctions; Douglas (2003), for example, explicitly studies ensembles in
relation to predictive power.

1.2 The Core Principle: Plenitude Is Proposal Generation
We formulate the core principle of this monograph:


                                                      1
                          Membership in an abundant representation space alone
             does not establish unique selection within a declared family and evidence record.

Vast representational plenitude does not defeat a theory mathematically. Instead, it places the theory in
a specific epistemic category: it is an abundant reservoir of candidate descriptions. But membership of a
specific candidate 𝑥 within a representation space 𝒮 does not confer physical authority upon 𝑥. Unique
model selection requires discrimination among declared alternatives. Identification of a target property is a
different question: several surviving models may agree on that property. Proposition 2.3 below states when
a property can be recovered from observations.

1.3 Scope
The results concern declared finite families, observation maps, and rational graph data. A family-relative se-
lection is conditional on the supplied alternatives, measurements, and tolerances. Measurement authenticity
and adequacy of the model family are separate questions.

2.4 Identifying a Property Without Selecting a Model
Proposition 2.3 (Observation Factorization). Let F be a declared family, 𝒪 ∶ 𝐹 → 𝐸 an observation
map, and 𝑃 ∶ 𝐹 → 𝑇 a target property. There exists 𝐷 ∶ 𝒪(𝐹 ) → 𝑇 with 𝑃 = 𝐷 ∘ 𝒪 if and only if


                                𝒪(𝑠) = 𝒪(𝑡) ⟹ 𝑃 (𝑠) = 𝑃 (𝑡)         (𝑠, 𝑡 ∈ 𝐹 ).

Proof. If 𝑃 = 𝐷 ∘ 𝒪, equality of observations implies equality of property values by substitution. Conversely,
for each 𝑒 ∈ 𝒪(𝐹 ) define 𝐷(𝑒) as the common value of 𝑃 on the nonempty fiber 𝒪−1 (𝑒). Fiber constancy
makes this value well-defined, and 𝐷(𝒪(𝑠)) = 𝑃 (𝑠). ■
Proof status: written proof; not mechanized in Lean. Recoverability is relative to the declared family and
observation map. It does not certify the family as exhaustive or the measurements as authentic.
In Scenario A of Section 5.1, both survivors have three generations, so that property is identified within the
survivor family even though the model is not uniquely selected. In Section 3, retaining only local acceptance
flags identifies neither the exact holonomy nor its vanishing: the zero cochain and an edge-simple false-
green witness have the same all-green profile but different holonomies. Holonomy is not constant on that
observation fiber, so Proposition 2.3 rules out its recovery from those flags alone.



3. The Local-Green Global-Red Boundary
Local tolerance checks need not determine whether edge data integrate to vertex values. The following graph
calculation gives a finite counterexample. Its application to a physical theory requires a separately justified
encoding.

3.1 Discrete Cochain Complexes of Local Evaluations
Consider a finite loop-free graph 𝐺 = (𝑉 , 𝐸) with one chosen stored orientation per undirected edge and no
duplicate edges. The graph may represent local observation patches or theoretical sectors only when that
correspondence is separately supplied.
A signed traversal (𝑢, 𝑣, 𝑠), 𝑠 ∈ {−1, +1}, is normalized to the stored edge and its orientation sign before
reading its cochain value or tolerance. Thus (𝑢, 𝑣, +1) and (𝑣, 𝑢, −1) describe the same step. Cochains and
tolerances are functions on exactly the stored-edge domain; extra reverse entries are rejected. A closed walk
may repeat edges. An edge-simple closed cycle visits each stored edge at most once. Evaluator soundness
and the per-traversal upper bound hold on closed walks; the false-green and sharpness constructions below
use edge-simple closed cycles.
• 𝐶 0 (𝑉 , ℚ) is the space of 0-cochains: global candidate field assignments 𝑓 ∶ 𝑉 → ℚ.

4. The Finite Terminal Selector
Having established that representation abundance inflates underdetermination and that local consistency
does not guarantee global realizability, we now formulate the operational mechanism required to establish
empirical selection.

4.3 The Non-Promotion Principle and Scope of Acceptance
Mathematical argument: extending a complete family with one further survivor changes its survivor

relative to any declared family 𝐹 and evidence record 𝑒.

It means that candidate 𝑠 is the sole survivor among the declared alternatives in 𝐹 that satisfies evidence

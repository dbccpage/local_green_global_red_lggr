Abstract
We compare two representations of a finite real cochain: its diagonal operator and the rank-one projector
obtained from its normalized signed square-root coordinates. The diagonal map is injective and preserves the
sum-of-absolute-values norm as trace norm. The projector identifies precisely the nonzero scalar multiples
of a cochain, losing mass and global orientation while retaining every diagnostic constant on those fibers.
Trace distance defines a pseudometric on nonzero cochains and a metric on their real projective quotient. We
prove a chain-complex residual bound, describe local reconstruction using an orientation anchor, and show
that a continuous global sign choice is impossible in dimension at least two. A guarded evaluator uses these
representations under an explicit selection policy. The real-vector arguments are written proofs; the Lean
package verifies narrower integer and rational identities. No physical quantum interpretation is asserted.
Why a researcher should care. A representation can preserve some questions and erase others. The
complete fiber description states which quantities can be recovered, which additional data reconstruction
needs, and why a shape distance cannot replace a scale-sensitive residual.

1. Introduction: Representation vs. Topology
Modern computational verification and automated reasoning often translate discrete, combinatorial problem
states into continuous or spectral vector spaces (embeddings, matrices, operator algebras). While continuous
representations enable gradient-based search, spectral analysis, and metric comparisons, they introduce the
epistemic risk of representation confusion: confusing a chosen continuous embedding with ground-truth
correctness.
This paper establishes the formal interface between discrete combinatorial cochains and continuous spectral
operators. We define two canonical mappings:
1. The Linear Trace Embedding 𝐹lin ∶ 𝐶 1 (𝑃 ; ℝ) → ℬ1 (ℋ𝐸 ), which preserves exact ℓ1 norm as operator
   trace norm and remains injective in the canonical basis.
2. The Nonlinear Density Lift 𝐹den ∶ 𝐶 1 (𝑃 ; ℝ) ∖ {0} → 𝒮(ℋ𝐸 ), which normalizes mass and identifies
   antipodal orientations to construct unit-trace rank-1 density matrices via the classical Mazur map 𝑀1,2 .
We establish the Lossy Collision Principle: the nonlinear density representation discards scalar magni-
tude and orientation sign, preventing reverse reconstruction of combinatorial configurations from spectral
projections alone without extrinsic mass and orientation data.

6. Scope of the Operator Construction (Physical Nonclaims)
Verification scope. This section is a negative boundary. It is not a proved theorem.
We establish the scope and interpretive boundaries governing all spectral and operator constructions:
1. Scope of Mathematical Representation: ℋ𝐸 is a finite-dimensional real Hilbert space auxiliary to
   combinatorial analysis, used strictly to compute trace distances. No system governed by this framework
   is claimed to represent a quantum mechanical physical system, wavefunction, or microscopic state.
2. Non-Physical Interpretation: The use of trace distance and density matrix algebra does not imply
   that discrete topological constraints emerge from quantum mechanics. The discrete graph topology 𝑃 is
   foundational; the operator construction is a subordinate computational tool.
3. Scope Statement Status: This section constitutes an explicit declaration of methodology and scope,
   not a physical theorem or empirical finding.

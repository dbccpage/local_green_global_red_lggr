# Contextuality: what it means, and where this work stands

A **context** is a set of measurements that can be performed together. The question is whether the probability tables for all the allowed contexts can be obtained by restricting one ordinary probability distribution over assignments of outcomes to every measurement. The measurements in that imagined assignment need not be jointly performable. Asking whether the assignment model exists is a testable mathematical question, not a demand for an observer outside the world.

In a Bell experiment, each context chooses one setting at each separated site. Each individual table can be normalized and nonnegative, and tables can agree on their shared marginals, while no setting-independent distribution over local response assignments reproduces them all. This is Bell nonlocality as a contextuality obstruction. “Local green, global red” is an apt description if we state exactly what is green and which global model is excluded. It does not mean nature is inconsistent. The global-section formulation was developed by Abramsky and Brandenburger in 2011: [primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1102.0264).

There are several related theories. Kochen–Specker contextuality studies compatible quantum measurements and context-independent value assignments. Bell locality adds a spatial separation and local-response structure. Generalized operational contextuality also compares preparations and transformations that cannot be distinguished by specified experiments. These definitions have different assumptions; everyday dependence on surrounding information is not automatically any of them. Budroni and colleagues review the definitions, experiments, graph methods, and applications: [2022 review, revised 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.13036).

Two measures used here ask different optimization questions. **Contextual fraction (CF)** asks how much of a behavior cannot be supplied by a nonnegative local subdistribution. **Minimum negative mass** allows signed weights over global assignments and minimizes their total negative part. Such weights are a mathematical representation, not negative observed frequencies. CF has an established resource-theoretic and Bell-witness interpretation: [Abramsky, Barbosa and Mansfield, 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.07918). Relations between contextuality measures in cyclic systems have also been studied, with conventions that must be matched before transferring formulas: [Camillo and Cervantes, 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16574).

Our GHZ example makes the distinctions tangible. Fixed X/Y measurements on
\(\cos t\,|000\rangle-\sin t\,|111\rangle\) give
\(\mathrm{CF}=2N_{\min}=\max(0,2\sin(2t)-1)\).
On the first rising interval, the state is entangled for every positive time but the measured behavior remains local through \(t=\pi/12\). At \(t=\pi/4\), CF reaches one. The GHZ X/Y endpoint with CF equal to one is already discussed in [Appendix B of the 2017 contextual-fraction paper](https://arxiv.org/html/1705.07918); it is not a discovery of this package. Entanglement, nonlocality of a particular experiment, and the ability to certify a violation through noise are different questions.

**Are we doing undergraduate work or advanced research?** The answer depends on the layer. A CHSH derivation and this small Hamiltonian calculation are suitable advanced undergraduate or introductory graduate exercises. Convex duality, contextuality resource theory, and quantum measurement theory are graduate material. Building a correct, reusable Lean development with explicit Born maps, real optimization proofs, and independently replayable certificates is substantial formal-methods work. A theorem count does not establish novelty.

The present result is best described as a formally checked synthesis with a concrete dynamical extension. We have not established priority for these family formulas or the elementary inverse-sine window. We have not proved a sharp comparison over the entire quantum set, nor shown that the earlier CF>2N counterexample is quantum. The factor-two question across all finite quantum Bell scenarios is now settled negatively by Section 8.4: a full three-setting experiment on one entangled qubit pair has CF=4N_min>2N_min. The sharp universal replacement bound and the earlier three-party table remain open here. Nearby current work includes [simplicial resource transformations (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24010) and [reported Lean CHSH rigidity (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03884); the latter is a stronger near-optimal-strategy classification problem and has not been independently replayed here. This places our work in a real research area without pretending that rediscovering its foundations is a new frontier.

Literature checked 23 October 2026. The comparison of sophistication is an editorial assessment; cited external proof developments are not part of this package's verification receipt.

The counterexample uses the established [chained Bell construction of Braunstein and Caves](https://www-users.york.ac.uk/~sb54/papers/90/bcB90.pdf). What this upgrade adds is an explicit full-table comparison, a quantum realization, and exact independently replayable and Lean-checked certificates. Changing the number of measurement settings changes the geometry; a CHSH proportionality factor cannot be assumed universal.

Why does the factor change? On our CHSH line, the deterministic scores range from -2 to 2 while the algebraic maximum is 4. The matched certificates give CF as the score excess divided by 2, and minimum negative mass as the same excess divided by 4. In the new chained line, the deterministic range is -4 to 4 and the algebraic maximum is 6: the corresponding denominators are 2 and 8. The ratio is therefore four. These equalities depend on the explicit matching certificates; score ranges alone provide bounds, not exact optima for arbitrary behaviors.

Version 0.11.0 answers the next question more carefully: factor four is also false for a complete quantum experiment, with an exact rational 25-context counterexample. Restricted cycle covers have unbounded CF/N as their size grows. The complete equally spaced tables do not have an unbounded large-setting ratio: additional CHSH subexperiments impose a nonzero negative-mass floor. Removing contexts changes the optimization problem. These results do not establish a sharp coefficient, or a finite universal coefficient, over every complete quantum Bell scenario. The established chained and local-content literature includes [Barrett, Kent and Pironio (2006)](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0605182).

## Complete scenarios (0.12.0)

The new smoothed Gram family separates two resource measures even with every setting pair included: CF tends to one while minimum negative mass tends to zero. This does not contradict the bounded regular planar-chain result. The ingredients are established contextual-fraction packing, chained witnesses and the Tsirelson unit-vector representation; no claim of priority is made for combining them. The written all-size quantum construction goes beyond the current end-to-end Lean coverage.
